Proposed work

Ten days, one defined question.

A buyer first identifies a real operational *problem* without sharing actual records. We jointly define a wholly fictionalized input and agreed criteria. This exercise would not replace, control or integrate with the buyer's existing warehouse software.

01 / Days 1–2

Define the exception

Identify one owner, expected decisions, boundaries and objective pass/fail criteria, all by email.

02 / Days 3–5

Build a fictional fixture

Set up isolated synthetic order/inventory cases and document simple allow, review and block rules.

03 / Days 6–8

Test and observe

Record approved and rejected scripted paths, invalid-input behavior, unexpected results and failures.

04 / Days 9–10

Deliver written findings

Provide results by test case, limitations, evidence/reproduction notes and a go/no-go recommendation.

What is verified

Internal prototype evidence, not a customer deployment.

The proprietary BOLT application is an early macOS prototype. Internal offline work evaluated 14 fabricated logistics events; five synthetic cases were passed into an isolated Swift test harness with mock providers and scripted approvals/rejections. These are developer tests, not third-party certification, field trials or verified savings.

  • No production integration: no WMS, ERP, EDI, inventory writes, order changes, carrier API or industrial equipment connection.
  • No real decisions: simulated approval does not mean an actual employee approved a customer operation.
  • No sensitive information: no production credentials, actual customer records, personal data or financial transactions in the initial exercise.
  • Independent example: the four-case browser illustration runs deterministic JavaScript, not the proprietary BOLT product or an AI inference model.
  • Legal readiness pending: Unfire is a founder-led pre-incorporation initiative in Spain. Any paid work depends on written agreement, confirmed supplier eligibility and resolving relevant Spanish tax, Social Security and unemployment-benefit requirements.
  • No guarantees: no certification, production SLA, liability terms, ROI, payment plan or customer results have been agreed or promised.
