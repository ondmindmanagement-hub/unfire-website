The idea

Not simply played.
Revealed.

Closed, Hyperwave 2000 is a sealed silver form under a sapphire canopy. At a touch it opens: the display rises, the keys arrive, the controls lift into the hand and two speakers reach outward. The transformation is not decoration added to an instrument. It is the instrument's first gesture, and the reason its future physical form demands precision robotics, fine craftsmanship and patient engineering.

The choreography

Six movements.

  1. 01At rest

    A silver body sealed beneath a translucent sapphire canopy. A single illuminated control on the front invites the first touch.Proposed physical form: precision-machined enclosure, sealed hinges, closed-state interlocks.

  2. 02The awakening

    The canopy lifts and turns about 92° to stand upright; the display within boots to life.Proposed: geared rotary drives with encoders, counterbalance and torque limiting.

  3. 03The keys emerge

    The keyboard tray rises and advances; twenty-five keys arrive in a staggered wave.Proposed: guided linear axes with a cam-sequenced key reveal over a playable keybed.

  4. 04The command surface

    The control panel lifts and inclines toward the player; nine rotary controls, wheels and ribbon take their places.Proposed: lift-and-tilt linkage; knobs that remain fully manual once deployed.

  5. 05Full expression

    Two speaker pods extend on telescopic arms and articulate outward around the musician.Proposed: one linear and one rotary axis per pod, with flex-rated audio cabling.

  6. 06Light and sound

    Light signatures, display and speakers come alive. The instrument is ready to be played.Proposed: motion control isolated from the low-latency audio engine.

Engineering
Why it cannot be
an ordinary keyboard.

    
On screen, geometry can stretch freely. In metal and glass, every movement needs a drive, a bearing, a sensor, a load path, a cable route and a safe way to stop. Reproducing Hyperwave's choreography faithfully — quietly, repeatably and safely around a musician's hands — is a robotics programme in its own right. That is a large part of its value, and the reason we will not shortcut it.

   

   

    
i.
Motors
Digital today
Lid, tray, panel, keys, controls and pods animate on scripted keyframes.
Physical proposal
Twelve quiet stepper axes in Unfire’s design v0.1 — keys and knobs are cam-synchronised, so twenty-five keys need one camshaft, not twenty-five motors.

    
ii.
Sensors
Digital today
Positions are known exactly in software.
Physical proposal
Absolute encoders, limit switches, current sensing and pinch detection on every moving assembly.

    
iii.
Articulations
Digital today
The canopy rotates about 92°; the panel lifts and inclines.
Physical proposal
Precision hinges and linkages sized from real mass and clearance, with counterbalance on the canopy.

    
iv.
Transmissions
Digital today
Keys arrive in a staggered wave; controls rise and turn.
Physical proposal
Camshafts, lead screws and gear trains — custom where the sequence demands it.

    
v.
Moving cabling
Digital today
Not represented.
Physical proposal
Flex circuits and cable chains rated for repeated cycles through hinges and telescopic arms.

    
vi.
Electronics
Digital today
A browser Web Audio engine and 3D renderer.
Physical proposal
Embedded audio processor, display, motor controllers and power system, verified for latency, heat and electromagnetic noise.

    
vii.
Audio
Digital today
A playable 32-voice virtual-analogue synthesizer.
Physical proposal
Real drivers and amplification inside moving pods, engineered against resonance and motor noise.

    
viii.
Synchronised control
Digital today
One timeline drives every movement in about 4.7 seconds.
Physical proposal
A dedicated motion controller that coordinates all axes, isolated from the audio engine; real timing may be slower.

    
ix.
Mechanical safety
Digital today
No physical forces.
Physical proposal
Force and current limits, stable power-off positions, manual recovery and assessment by qualified conformity specialists.

   

   

    
Engineered with known mechanisms
  • Counterbalanced, geared canopy with brake
  • Two-track cam for the keyboard tray
  • Lead-screw panel lift on scissor guides
  • Four-stage telescopic speaker arms

    
Custom transmissions designed
  • 25-lobe camshaft for the key wave
  • Helix-and-slot bar: nine knobs rise, turn once, then turn freely
  • Wrist turntable inside each speaker pod

    
Corrected for real physics
  • Speaker pods now dock outside the body instead of passing through it
  • Stretching struts replaced by rigid telescopes and scissors
  • No part bounces or overshoots

   

   
Unfire engineering design v0.1: every movement of the digital instrument was audited for collisions and then corrected, with zero interferences measured across the whole transformation. Twelve-axis motion firmware with interlocks and pinch, stall and power-loss handling was verified in simulation. Not yet built, measured or certified.
