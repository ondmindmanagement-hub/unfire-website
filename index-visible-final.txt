AI · Automation · Computer workflows
Bolt
Bolt
explores controlled AI orchestration for complex computer work. It is designed to coordinate AI systems, local tools and execution processes while preserving visibility, permissions and human control.
The project investigates how repetitive interaction between people, models and software can be reduced by allowing a validated plan to continue through execution, testing and repair cycles with fewer unnecessary interruptions.

Energy · Data · Information systems
Power
Power explores how complex electricity and energy-market information can be converted into software that is easier to understand, compare and use.
The project combines regional data, pricing information and structured interpretation into a clearer interface, creating a foundation for future energy intelligence tools.
The same approach can later support other information-heavy products where fragmented external data needs to become understandable and actionable software.

is the first product developed within the broader

It is a precision audio clipping tool built around Gain, Threshold and Atmosphere, designed to give producers and engineers direct control over clipping behaviour, peaks, transients and sonic character.

System
Software production without starting from scratch.
The central idea is simple: when a new product can use technology that already exists inside
Unfire
, development should reuse that advantage instead of rebuilding it.
Shared foundation
Reusable software components, production knowledge and validation systems form a base that can continue evolving.
Product layer
New applications can combine existing capabilities with product-specific technology rather than beginning from an empty architecture.
Validation
Builds, tests and internal checkpoints provide measurable evidence of whether software candidates are functioning as intended.
Expansion
Infrastructure can grow through additional compute, storage, testing, quality assurance and deployment capacity.
Production scale
The bottleneck is shifting.
Where the existing architecture applies, internal development has demonstrated that working software candidates can move through extremely short build cycles — in some cases measured in minutes.
From rebuilding software to scaling infrastructure.
As reusable technology expands, the limiting factors increasingly become infrastructure resources such as compute, storage, testing, quality assurance and commercial deployment — resources that can themselves be expanded.
