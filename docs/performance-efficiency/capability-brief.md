# Unfire Performance & AI Efficiency

**Exploratory capability brief · 30 October 2026 · Research / pilot stage**

Unfire is an independent, founder-led technology initiative exploring more reliable and efficient ways to develop software and run human-supervised AI workflows. We seek a small number of properly scoped academic evaluations and potential enterprise pilot discussions. **This is not an announcement of a commercially available performance-boosting product.**

## Problems we want to measure

- **Development efficiency:** repeated compilations, build bottlenecks and development-tool overhead.
- **AI workflow efficiency:** unnecessary model calls, context duplication, retry loops and token expenditure, without degrading output quality.
- **Computer health:** observable bottlenecks in available disk space, resource contention and application responsiveness, using read-only diagnostics.
- **Reliable automation:** human approvals, recoverability, transparent estimated costs and traceable task execution.

## What exists today

- A local, read-only Unfire Performance Lab prototype, with internally recorded, source-specific compiler-cache benchmarks, a performance dashboard and storage review. Its measurements are **not** validation of whole-product speed or AI-model acceleration.
- An internally tested BOLT prototype, with human-authorisation and estimated-cost concepts. Enterprise deployment, cross-platform availability, security certification and independent field validation **are not established by this brief**.
- A preliminary research protocol for measuring speed, quality, cost and errors against a controlled baseline.

## What we propose to evaluate

1. Choose **one non-confidential, repeatable workflow** with a narrow success definition.
2. Establish the baseline on the same system and data, recording task completion, p50/p95 latency, errors and resource consumption.
3. Apply one reversible, consented optimisation at a time, while preserving output quality and operator control.
4. Repeat tests, report both positive and negative results, and recommend whether any further pilot is justified.

For any real-world client engagement, security, software-distribution permissions, data processing, legal contracting and costs must first be reviewed. We do not ask partners to disable operating-system security.

## Engagement routes

**Universities / research centres:** faculty-led, synthetic-data research questions; methodology and academic independence remain with the institution.

**Companies:** exploratory diagnostic conversation by email, followed *only if feasible* by a clearly defined, mutually approved pilot with pre-agreed acceptance criteria. Licensing, integration or support can be evaluated separately; no pricing or return-on-investment claim is made at this stage.
